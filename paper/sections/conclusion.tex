\section{Conclusion}

Boundary Density Likelihood addresses a common mismatch in time-series detection: models are often trained as samplewise segmenters, but evaluated by the timing of ranked detections.
It converts annotations into expected events on the model output timeline and fits the corresponding conditional mean with a Poisson log score.
Across the sleep and CHB-MIT benchmarks, changing this training signal improves event AP under matched architectures and post-processing.
On sleep, the gain is largest at strict matching tolerances, persists across multiple backbones, and is not reproduced by segmentation reweighting or smoothing choices alone.
More broadly, time-discretized sequence models do not have to inherit the assumptions of samplewise segmentation.
When a system is judged by ranked event times, its training signal should estimate the events that will later be matched; when it is judged by per-sample decisions, state labels remain the direct supervision.
BDL keeps the practical advantages of sequence prediction while making the model output estimate the temporal detections used by event-level metrics.
